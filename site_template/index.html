This is not a medical tool. The data is 768 women of Pima heritage aged 21 and over, collected near Phoenix, Arizona and published in 1988. The model has not been tested on anyone else. It is here to show how a risk model is built and judged, not to assess anyone's health.

Key findings

  • The original 81% was luck. Repeating the original method on 200 random train/test splits gives anything from {{lucky_min}} to {{lucky_max}}, averaging {{lucky_mean}}. The split in the college notebook beat {{lucky_beat}} of the others. Always answering "no diabetes" scores 65%.
  • Half the insulin column was never measured, and it can't be rescued. {{insulin_n}} of 768 insulin values and {{skin_n}} skinfold values are zeros standing in for "missing". Six ways of filling them all score between {{strat_lo}} and {{strat_hi}}. When known insulin readings are hidden, the best method recovers only {{rec_insulin}} of their variation, and {{flips}} women ({{flips_pct}}) get a different screening decision depending on how the gaps are filled. So the published model leaves insulin and skinfold out, and scores the same without them.
  • The simplest model ties for best. With all eight inputs on the same 50 cross-validation splits, logistic regression scores AUC {{auc_lr}} and a random forest {{auc_rf}}, a gap one tenth the size of the wobble between splits. Gradient boosting ({{auc_gb}}) and a decision tree ({{auc_dt}}) are behind. Glucose alone gets {{auc_glu}}.
  • Accuracy hid the real problem. At the standard 0.50 cut-off the model misses {{fn_default}} of 268 women with diabetes. Moving the cut-off to {{cut}} catches {{sens_screen}} of them, at the price of {{fp_screen}} false alarms instead of {{fp_default}}.
  • The probabilities can be believed. Predicted risk tracks what happened (calibration slope {{cal_slope}}, ideal 1.00), so "30%" does mean roughly 3 in 10, for women like these.
  • It works worst for older women. With one cut-off for everyone, the model clears only {{spec_old}} of healthy women aged 45 and over. It flags nearly all of them. That is the main thing the next milestones set out to fix.
